The site

A fenced yard with trees and 2 shipping containers, and a warehouse with 3 entrances: the main door (south), a side door (east) and a dock door that opens onto a raised loading dock. The dock and the mezzanine behind it are 1.4 m up and reached by 3 staircases: 2 outside, 1 inside. Windows line the walls.

Drones fly at 2.6 to 3.2 m. They pass over the racks and the dock, but must route through the doors and around tree canopies and containers. The intruder walks, so walls, racks, trunks, containers and platform edges block them, and they use the stairs.

How the drones decide

There is no central controller. Each drone runs the same planner. Every 0.5 s it builds the task list from what it knows: barrier slots in front of the intruder, search sectors that still have unexplored cells, and marking a found asset.

It bids a cost (seconds to reach) for every task and broadcasts the bids. Then it runs the same greedy allocation on all the bids it has heard: barrier slots first, then the cheapest drone-to-sector pairs. Because every drone uses the same rule on the same data, they agree without talking to a leader.

Radio is imperfect. Messages are lost or out of range, so two drones can claim the same task. When a drone hears a claim with a lower cost than its own, it yields and re-plans. Those moments are counted as conflicts.

The swarm chooses how many drones to pull off the search: 2 for a far intruder, 3 when the intruder is within 12 m of a door or when there is nothing left to search. A single drone can be walked around; 2 side by side block a corridor or a doorway. The intruder heads for the door with the shortest walk, switches to another door after 3.5 s without progress, and gives up after 12 s in total.
