RESEARCH BASIS · NOVEMBER 2026

A flying robot needs more
than a large model.

Recommended first research configuration: a PX4-class flight controller, an Orin NX 16 GB companion computer, a calibrated RGB-D camera and a compact VLA adapted to drone velocity actions. An X650-class airframe provides more payload margin for cooling, power conversion and a gentle contact probe than a small vision drone.

This additional simulator runs exactly one quadcopter in the browser. The current autonomous policy is a real, compact neural network trained on synthetic flight examples, with explicit task logic, simulated visual sensing and geometric flight stabilization. No foundation VLA checkpoint is loaded. Selecting a VLA in the hardware panel estimates its memory budget.

The common architecture is camera + language + vehicle state → short flight action → fast autopilot → motor forces. PX4 supports position, velocity and other setpoints from a companion computer; it requires continued offboard-control health and appropriate loss handling. See PX4 Offboard mode. The simulator uses its own controller, not installed PX4 firmware.

What is actually running

  • One 6-DoF Cannon ES rigid body, four independently applied rotor forces and yaw torques, motor lag, mass/inertia, gravity, wind drag and battery consumption. Flight control and physics run at 240 Hz.
  • A front or underslung camera, adjustable pitch/yaw/FOV, actual rendered RGB, a switchable inspection light, click-to-select visual references and synchronized image/state/action export.
  • A 387-parameter 8 → 32 → 3 MLP trained on 45,000 seeded synthetic flight examples. It predicts translational velocity; geometry supervises routing and ranges, while a conventional flight controller stabilizes attitude and mixes rotors. Held-out velocity MAE is about 0.113 m/s on this synthetic distribution.
  • Known test-bay geometry for routing; target state becomes available only through simulated camera FOV/occlusion observations. Semantic labels and depth are simulator outputs. Typed local instructions use an explicit small parser, not a foundation language model.
  • Find requires sustained visual confirmation. Inspect requires an observed stand-off and dwell. Gentle touch requires the selected fixture to contact the probe at ≤0.25 m/s closing speed; approach speed is capped at 0.12 m/s. A hull/rotor collision or contact with another fixture stops the episode.
  • Manual override, hold, pause, single-step, payload checks and stale-action braking. Inferencing and flight stabilization are separate. Long or missing external inference does not freeze physics.

Touch is a fixture-inspection task. It needs near-field sensing and contact feedback, not only a bounding box. For example, OAK-D S2 depth limits depend on camera settings; the documented minimum range is approximately 20–80 cm. A short probe, near-range sensor and/or compliant force sensor should be included in the real design.

A real VLA adapter boundary

Select External drone-trained VLA and provide an HTTP(S) endpoint with CORS permission for this page. The browser sends POST packets containing an episode/mission/sequence, capture time and camera calibration, PNG RGB, selected reference crop, instruction, current local pose, ranges and explicitly marked simulated semantic observations. The adapter returns:

{
  "version": 1,
  "episode": 1,
  "mission": 2,
  "sequence": 3,
  "frame": "body_flu",
  "velocity_mps": [0.2, 0.0, 0.0],
  "yaw_rate_rps": 0.0,
  "duration_s": 0.2
}

FLU means forward, left, up. Speed must be ≤1.5 m/s, yaw rate ≤1 rad/s and duration ≤0.5 s. Identity mismatches, non-finite data, unknown frames and responses older than 0.75 simulated seconds are rejected. Values are transformed through the measured attitude; the onboard flight controller and obstacle/range supervisor remain active. No arm joint index is interpreted as a rotor command.

The downloadable recording stores current RGB and camera pose, instruction, observed objects, state, velocity action and four actual forces. It is a bounded JSON research dataset (latest 1,000 records), not already a LeRobot dataset. A next training phase would collect varied expert trajectories, convert data to a flight-specific training schema, fit/fine-tune the action policy, and evaluate held-out layouts, camera mounts, payloads, lighting and delay. Any successful simulator policy still needs vehicle-specific calibration and staged flight validation.
